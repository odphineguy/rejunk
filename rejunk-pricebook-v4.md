## Photo Rules (MANDATORY — from Sam's operational playbook)

**These apply to every quoting interaction, whether API auto-response or manual.**

1. **Junk removal:** ALWAYS ask for photos before confirming a final price. Analyze photos to estimate weight (landfill charges per ton), volume (truck space), and identify hazardous/heavy items.
2. **Moves (all types):** ALWAYS ask for photos of the space — storage units, apartments, homes. Check for excessively packed areas, oversized items, narrow hallways, and stair situations that would require extra time, equipment, or crew.
3. **Assembly (large/complex items):** Ask for a photo or link to the product listing so the worker arrives with the right tools and time expectation.
4. **Initial quote = estimate until photos confirm.** Response template:
```
Based on your description, this would typically run around $[X]. Could you send 
a few photos so I can confirm the exact price? That way there are no surprises 
for either of us.
```

## Scope Change Protocol

**Any work not in original quote = STOP → re-quote → customer accepts → then work.**

Auto-responder: "Absolutely — let me get you a quick updated quote with the additional items."
NEVER: "Sure, no problem" / "We'll take care of it" / "Yeah we can grab that too."

---

## Quoting Workflow (Sam's rules merged with ours)

1. Map the Thumbtack lead description to the closest Pricebook line item. If multiple items, sum them.
2. Quote the flat rate. If the job clearly exceeds one line (e.g. "4+ pickup trucks"), scale or ask for photos.
3. When the Pricebook doesn't have a clean match, ask the customer rather than guessing.
4. ALWAYS ask about stairs on moves. Apply stair surcharges ($100/$200/$300 per floor).
5. ALWAYS ask for photos of junk hauls before confirming final price. Analyze to estimate weight, space, and hazards.
6. ALWAYS ask for photos of moves (storage units, apartments, homes) to assess scope, packed areas, and time.
7. If confidence is low, quote a range, not a fixed price.
